## 2. The Yagé-ocó naming decision

**The old site contradicts itself.**

- `/master-plants/` prints: Yagé-ocó = *Diplopterys cabrerana*, heirloom variety "Yají",
  "originally from the Wambiza tribe."
- `/2023/06/25/the-delicate-nature-of-ayahuasca-and-yage/` prints: "*Diplopterys cabrerana*
  known as 'chagropanga', 'chali panga' or 'yagéocó'."
- `/2024/03/31/the-yage-complex-by-neil-logan/` prints: "Yagé oco = *Diplopterys longialata*
  var. *huambisa*", and carries a section titled "*Diplopterys cabrerana* Confusion" arguing
  that *cabrerana* is the misapplication.

Note that "Wambiza" on the plants page and "huambisa" in Logan's article are the same word
(the Wampís / Huambisa people). The plants page is already carrying half of Logan's
identification attached to the other name.

**Decision for the new site:**

> **Yagé-ocó · Yají** — *Diplopterys longialata* (Huambisa type)
> Commonly published as *Diplopterys cabrerana*; the two are closely similar and are
> frequently conflated.

Reasoning:
1. It is Jonathon's own stated position (recorded meeting, July 2026).
2. It matches the more recent and more rigorous of the two articles already on his site.
3. *Diplopterys longialata* (Nied.) W.R.Anderson & C.Davis is a validly accepted species
   at Kew/POWO — this is not an invented name.
4. **"var. huambisa" is dropped.** Huambisa is a people and a trade name, not a validly
   published botanical variety; several vendors attach "Huambisa" to *cabrerana*. Printing it
   in italics as formal taxonomy is the one element a botanist could call an error.
5. *D. cabrerana* stays visible on the page, so the site is not concealing the mainstream name.

This matches Jonathon's instruction on the recording: present the information without
declaring the debate resolved.

**Still needs Jonathon's sign-off** — see `JONATHON-QUESTIONS.md` item 1.

## 4. Dieta protocol — full text

Source page: https://rainforestmedicine.net/dieta-protocol/

This is a safety document. Nothing in it may be dropped, softened, or summarised away on
the full page. The homepage carries a short version that links here.

### 4.1 Sexual activity

**Before ceremony.** In traditional Siekopai initiations celibacy was required at the onset of
training. Among couples the guidelines are less strict, but energy management remains
important: sexual energy is to be conserved and allowed to rise within the body rather than
be depleted.

**After ceremony.** Duration depends on the intensity of the experience:
- **Minimum 1 week** — following powerful visions or a profound healing breakthrough.
- **1+ months** — for those healing from illness.
- **1+ years** — for disciples undergoing advanced graduation-level ceremonies.

Maintaining calm sexual energy allows it to build and rise through the body, supporting
spiritual development and preventing the *pinta* (spiritual essence) from dissipating.

### 4.2 Fasting — day of ceremony

- Complete fast: no food or water on the day of ceremony.
- If necessary, only water or a light tea before noon.
- Rationale: "the body be dry in order to more easily absorb the medicine."
- Avoid visiting other people's homes (risk of social obligation to eat); stay home or in nature.
- Avoid direct sun, physical work, socialising, excessive activity.
- View flowers and landscapes; arts, crafts, rest, meditation, prayer.
- Principle: "to do less is to do more."

### 4.3 Food — avoid for 14 days before

- Alcoholic beverages of all kinds, including wine and beer
- Aged cheeses
- Refined white sugar
- Fried and heavy foods
- Fermented foods
- Processed foods
- All canned foods
- Spicy foods and hot chili
- Garlic and onions
- Pork and processed meats
- All dairy products

### 4.4 Herbs and supplements — avoid for 9 days before

- St. John's wort
- Passionflower
- Valerian root
- Lemon balm
- Ginseng
- Nutmeg
- Kratom
- Kava

**Cannabis / marijuana** — not strictly prohibited, but abstention is recommended in order to
more fully experience the medicine. A personal choice, though many people overuse it.

### 4.5 Medications — absolutely prohibited, 2+ months before

Risk includes **serotonin syndrome**.

- Antidepressants (Zoloft, Prozac, Celexa, Lexapro, Paxil, Luvox, Oleptro, and all others)
- Antipsychotics
- Antihypertensives (high blood pressure medication)
- Antihistamines
- Appetite suppressants / diet pills
- Asthma inhalers
- Barbiturates
- CNS depressants (Xanax, Ativan and similar)
- MAOIs
- Medications for asthma, bronchitis, breathing problems
- Cold, sinus, hay fever and allergy medications — Actifed DM, Benadryl, Benylin,
  Chlor-Trimeton, Compoz, Bromarest-DM/-DX, Dimetane-DX, Dristan Cold & Flu, Phenergan with
  Dextromethorphan, Robitussin-DM, Vicks Formula 44-D, Tylenol cold products, and any drug
  with "Tuss" in the name
- SSRIs
- Vasodilators

### 4.6 Drugs — fatal risk, 2+ months abstinence, disqualifying

Participation will be denied to anyone currently taking:

- Amphetamines (methamphetamine, dexamphetamine)
- Cocaine
- Dextromethorphan (DXM)
- Ephedrine
- MDMA (ecstasy)
- MDA
- MDEA
- PMA
- Opiates (heroin, morphine, codeine, opium)

### 4.7 Medical contraindications

**Menstruation and ejaculation.** Women who are menstruating, and men who have had a wet
dream or ejaculated in the preceding days, cannot participate or assist with post-ceremony
breakfast preparation. Medically, menstruating women risk prolific haemorrhaging, and the
detoxification pressure of the medicine can become dangerous. In the tradition this energy is
related to the physical dimension of life on earth and prevents celestial spirits from
entering the lodge; the facilitator sees blood dripping from the roof edges, and the spirits
perceive the ceremonial lodge as an area of blurred red.

**Pregnancy.** Pregnant women do not drink yagé. They may attend at dawn to receive healing
from the facilitators. Men whose partners are pregnant must bring their own cup — drinking
from a shared cup passes too much energy to the foetus — and should ask for help tying
hammocks, as otherwise the foetus can become tangled in the umbilical cord.

**Children.** Under the age of 9 do not drink yagé; they may attend at dawn for healing.

**Asthma.** Research the specific inhaler medication — some are contraindicated with the
MAOIs present in yagé.

**Diabetes.** Extra risk. The MAOIs present in yagé can alter blood sugar levels through
hypoglycaemic effects and may change insulin or oral medication requirements. Severe or
unstable diabetes: should not participate. Less severe: must contact the organisers before
drinking to discuss a possible modified dieta.

**Epilepsy and seizure history.** Inform the organisers if the participant or an immediate
family member has a history of epilepsy or seizures. If not properly managed and monitored,
yagé can induce a seizure where there is prior family history.

**Heart and cardiovascular.** It is not advisable to participate with any chronic heart
condition or high blood pressure. Yagé can elevate blood pressure and creates serious and/or
fatal risk to those with serious heart conditions or high blood pressure. Pacemaker users are
excluded. Those with less serious heart issues may discuss participation further.

**Hypothyroidism.** Generally safe to partake. Must inform the organisers so dosage can be
closely monitored. Synthetic thyroid hormones such as Synthroid may be continued through the
ceremony without complication.

**Liver and kidney.** Yagé use can be stressful for the liver and kidneys, which metabolise
harmine and related alkaloids. Accumulated monoamines from diet, environment, medical
conditions or pharmaceutical/illicit drugs add further metabolic stress.

### 4.8 During ceremony

**1 — Comfort and no technology.** Be sure of your spot before the ceremony begins; ask to
move beforehand if uncomfortable. Do not move once it begins. No recording or photography,
during the ceremony or the following morning — recording freezes a very dynamic moment; live
the experience so it becomes part of you. No headphone music.

**2 — Remain in the ceremony area.** Do not leave the ceremonial area until dawn, and remain
until the ceremony fully concludes the following day. The mind can play tricks; the medicine's
effects may return after leaving the protected space, leaving participants vulnerable and
frightened, and many other things can occur. The ceremony concludes when the ceremonial
sceptre and the leafy broom rattle are placed back on the altar.

**3 — Stillness and silence.** Remain very still and quiet throughout — this is how to make
the most of the medicine and receive visions and spiritual transmissions. Refrain from
emotional outbursts unless totally uncontrollable. Do not interact with other participants.
Walk slowly if using the bathroom and cover your flashlight. Do not bump into others'
hammocks. No smoking inside the ceremonial area. Vomiting is acceptable — let it out, right
there if need be, it is part of the healing process — then return to quietude and meditation.
When negative thoughts arise, refrain from blaming the medicine, the facilitators or anyone
else; the medicine is showing us the parts of ourselves we have difficulty seeing. Cultivate
an unattached perspective, be present with the medicine, and surrender.

**4 — No food or drink.** Do not drink water unless very dry, and then only a few sips at
most. Water interacts with the time the medicine takes to act and interrupts the body's
process of absorbing and making sense of the yagé; it dilutes the medicine and causes the
body to absorb it too quickly, going straight to the brain, which can be maddening and highly
uncomfortable, to the point where people scream and disrupt the ceremony. The fast continues
through the ceremony.

**5 — No judgement, antagonism or expectation.** Trust the process. Judgements of any type —
of oneself, of others in the group, or of the facilitators — hold back the energy of the
ceremony. Be like a leaf in the wind; try not to resist anything. Come with beginner's eyes
and a calm, awake presence. You are in a safe and protected environment among wholehearted
people who care for you. Non-resistance makes the medicine much more effective. Attachments
are spiritual entrapments.

### 4.9 After

**The morning after — make no commitments.** People get animated and feel supernatural and
commit to all kinds of things that seem easy in the moment; later it dawns on them that it is
not that easy and they break their word. Wait until you have digested it and are sure of the
direction, then commit.

**Integration.** Allow the experience in, let it unfold, be curious. Remain still with it.
Keep it to oneself. Suspend attaching meaning — let the visions reveal their true meaning over
time, rather than getting caught in a barrier of form and symbolism. Visions are an energetic
transmission, given to give you energy. Rather than discussing visionary detail, demonstrate
understanding by upholding a universal culture of service and doing whatever one does in an
absolute way. If you need help interpreting experiences or making sense of things, seek a
person you can speak with in confidence.

### 4.10 General conduct during the dieta period

Practise non-reactionary responses, non-impulsive behaviour and patience. Refrain from
emotional outbursts, from casting judgement, from being overly opinionated, and from jumping
to conclusions. Refraining from aggravated emotional states allows body, mind and spirit to
begin to integrate. Let all one's relationships, in whatever form they take, be kind, mutual,
sincere and harmonious — part of a higher harmonious purpose.

### 4.11 Facilitator discretion

The ceremony facilitators have full discretion to cancel participation in further ceremonies
if a participant chooses to disregard any of these instructions.
